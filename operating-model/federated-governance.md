## SC4LE Federated Governance

Federated Governance is a core component of the SC4LE Operating Model.

It distributes decision-making to the level with the strongest relevant context while maintaining strategic coherence through shared principles, clear authority boundaries and appropriate guardrails.

Within SC4LE, the Central Design Authority (CDA), Local Design Authorities (LDAs) and operational teams work together to enable decisions to be made as close to the work as practical.

This model reduces bottlenecks, supports flow and strengthens ethical and regulatory alignment without creating unnecessary bureaucracy.

### Purpose of Federated Governance

Federated Governance exists to:

- enable faster and clearer decision-making
- reduce unnecessary escalation and dependency on central functions
- maintain alignment across teams, domains and value streams
- support autonomy without fragmentation
- strengthen ethical, regulatory and risk controls
- protect end-to-end flow
- use sensing to identify emerging governance issues
- create a scalable governance model for AI-enabled organisations

Federated Governance replaces slow, unnecessarily centralised governance with a principle-driven and distributed system.

### Core Components of Federated Governance

Federated Governance operates through six interconnected components.

#### 1. Shared Principles and Guardrails

Shared principles provide the alignment mechanism that enables locally made decisions to remain coherent across the wider organisation.

They:

- guide contextual judgement
- reduce ambiguity
- reduce cognitive load
- support consistent behaviour
- reduce reliance on heavy process
- establish boundaries within which authority is exercised

Principles and guardrails support local authority without removing accountability.

#### 2. Central Design Authority

The Central Design Authority (CDA) is the primary governance capability within SC4LE.

The CDA maintains enterprise-level and cross-domain coherence by:

- stewarding the interpretation and application of shared principles
- maintaining ethical and organisational guardrails
- owning enterprise-level and systemic decisions
- supporting LDAs
- managing material cross-domain matters
- reviewing systemic risks and dependencies
- integrating insights from AI-enabled sensing
- maintaining the effectiveness of the federated governance model

The CDA is a component of SC4LE and is not synonymous with SC4LE itself.

The CDA is enabling rather than controlling. It does not micromanage operational delivery or unnecessarily reclaim decisions from LDAs and teams.

#### 3. Local Design Authorities

Local Design Authorities (LDAs) are embedded governance capabilities that interpret shared principles within a defined organisational context.

LDAs:

- make contextual decisions
- exercise delegated authority
- support operational teams
- manage local risks, dependencies and trade-offs
- maintain alignment with shared guardrails
- collaborate with other LDAs where matters cross boundaries
- escalate only when established thresholds are reached
- integrate sensing signals into local governance

LDAs are lightweight, empowered and positioned close to the work.

LDAs must not act as smaller versions of the CDA or recreate centralised governance within individual domains.

#### 4. Operational Teams

Operational teams are the primary units of value delivery and day-to-day decision-making.

Teams:

- make local operational decisions within agreed boundaries
- apply shared principles through normal work
- maintain flow and quality
- identify risks, dependencies and delays
- participate in sensing and feedback loops
- contribute to continuous improvement

Operational teams are not passive recipients of governance. They exercise authority within their context and provide the operational signals needed for the governance system to learn.

#### 5. Decision Pathways

Decision pathways provide clarity about:

- who holds authority
- how decisions are made
- which guardrails apply
- when collaboration is required
- when escalation is required
- what evidence is proportionate
- how sensing informs decisions
- when decisions and rationale must be recorded

Decision pathways reduce ambiguity, delay and rework without creating unnecessary process.

#### 6. AI-Enabled Sensing

AI-enabled sensing provides early insight into:

- flow interruption
- decision latency
- repeated escalation
- governance friction
- risk signals
- dependencies
- systemic bottlenecks
- ethical or regulatory concerns

Sensing supports governance without replacing human judgement, decision ownership or accountability.

Sensing should remain lightweight, transparent, proportionate and actionable.

### How Federated Governance Works

#### 1. Decisions Are Made Close to the Work

Operational teams and LDAs make decisions using shared principles, relevant evidence and contextual expertise.

Authority should remain at the lowest appropriate level.

#### 2. Principles Guide Alignment

Shared principles and guardrails provide a stable basis for decision-making across different contexts.

When uncertainty arises, the relevant authority applies judgement using the principles, intended outcomes and available evidence.

#### 3. Authority Boundaries Provide Clarity

Teams, LDAs and the CDA operate within defined authority boundaries.

These boundaries establish:

- the scope of local decision rights
- relevant guardrails
- collaboration expectations
- escalation thresholds
- accountability requirements

#### 4. Escalation Is the Exception

Escalation occurs only when an established threshold is reached.

Examples include:

- risk exceeding local tolerance
- effects extending across multiple domains or value streams
- material ethical or regulatory exposure
- systemic consequences
- unresolved cross-domain dependencies
- material tensions that cannot be resolved within local authority

Escalation should transfer the unresolved element of a decision rather than unnecessarily transferring ownership of the entire matter.

#### 5. Sensing Informs Governance

AI-enabled feedback loops highlight emerging risks, governance friction, flow constraints and repeated decision patterns.

These insights inform proportionate intervention and improvement.

#### 6. Governance Evolves Continuously

Federated Governance evolves through sensing, feedback and learning.

Improvement normally focuses on:

- decision pathways
- authority boundaries
- governance mechanisms
- escalation thresholds
- sensing arrangements
- operating rhythms
- organisational capability

Governance should evolve through proportionate improvement rather than repeated large-scale redesign.

### Relationship Between SC4LE, the CDA and LDAs

SC4LE means Strategic Centre 4 Leading Enablement.

SC4LE is the overarching enablement system described by the SC4LE Operating Model.

Within that system:

- the CDA provides enterprise-level and systemic governance capability
- LDAs provide contextual and domain-level governance capability
- operational teams exercise local authority and deliver value
- AI-enabled sensing connects operational signals with governance and improvement
- enablement develops sustainable organisational capability

SC4LE should not be described as another name for the CDA.

The CDA is one of the capabilities through which SC4LE maintains coherence across a federated system.

### Benefits of Federated Governance

Federated Governance supports:

- faster and clearer decisions
- reduced rework and unnecessary escalation
- lower cognitive load
- stronger ethical and regulatory alignment
- greater autonomy for teams and domains
- clearer authority boundaries
- improved flow and delivery predictability
- earlier identification of systemic risks
- scalable governance for AI-enabled organisations
- continuous organisational adaptation

---

### Federated Governance Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- re-centralising decisions through unnecessary committees
- treating SC4LE and the CDA as synonymous
- creating LDAs without clear purpose, authority or guardrails
- allowing LDAs to operate as smaller versions of the CDA
- escalating decisions that remain within local authority
- reclaiming delegated authority without material cause
- over-engineering governance artefacts and processes
- allowing shared principles to become vague or disconnected from decisions
- using governance to control rather than enable
- treating sensing as optional reporting
- using sensing to police individuals or replace human accountability
- optimising local governance at the expense of wider system flow

### Summary

Federated Governance enables organisations to operate with autonomy, alignment and speed.

By combining shared principles, a Central Design Authority, empowered Local Design Authorities, operational teams, clear decision pathways and AI-enabled sensing, SC4LE creates a governance system that is coherent, adaptable and scalable.

The model maintains alignment without unnecessarily centralising control and supports governance that enables flow rather than constraining it.
