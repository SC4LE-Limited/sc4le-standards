# SC4LE Operating Model

## Purpose

The Operating Model layer defines how the SC4LE Principles are expressed through governance, authority, decision-making, delivery, sensing and continuous improvement.

It provides the structures, capabilities and operating mechanisms that enable organisations to apply SC4LE consistently while maintaining federated autonomy, organisational coherence and continuous adaptation.

The Operating Model translates the intent established by the Foundations layer into practical organisational capability.

---

## Relationship to the Foundations Layer

The Operating Model derives its intent and design constraints from the approved Foundations artefacts:

- `foundations/sc4le-narrative-architecture.md`
- `foundations/sc4le-principles.md`
- `foundations/principle-interpretation-guide.md`
- `foundations/README.md`

The Foundations layer defines:

- the SC4LE worldview
- the SC4LE Principles
- the interpretation of those Principles
- the relationships between concepts

The Operating Model does not redefine Foundations.

The Operating Model is the primary structural expression of the SC4LE Principles operating together as a coherent system.

Where conflicts exist, Foundation artefacts take precedence.

---

## Operating Model Architecture

The SC4LE Operating Model consists of five integrated layers:

1. Strategic Direction and Outcomes
2. Principles and Ethical Guardrails
3. Federated Governance
4. Operational Delivery
5. AI-Enabled Sensing and Continuous Improvement

These layers operate together as a system.

They are not separate organisational silos or sequential implementation stages.

The Operating Model exists to balance:

- alignment and autonomy
- coherence and contextual judgement
- strategic direction and local decision-making
- governance and flow
- delivery and learning
- human accountability and AI-enabled insight

## Authoritative Artefacts

The authoritative Operating Model artefact is:

`sc4le-operating-model-standard.md`

This standard defines:

- operating-model architecture
- governance structures
- authority relationships
- operating rhythms
- enablement model
- sensing model
- maturity relationships
- conformance requirements

Supporting artefacts expand, apply or visualise the model but must remain aligned with the standard.

Where a supporting Operating Model artefact conflicts with the Operating Model Standard, the standard takes precedence unless a Foundation artefact governs the matter.

## Governance and Ownership

The Central Design Authority (CDA) owns and stewards the Operating Model layer.

Responsibilities include:

- maintaining architectural coherence
- maintaining alignment with Foundations
- approving structural changes
- reviewing cross-artefact impacts
- maintaining terminology consistency
- governing operating-model evolution
- ensuring alignment between governance, delivery, sensing and improvement capabilities

Changes must follow the repository’s approved governance, discovery, impact-assessment and review processes.
