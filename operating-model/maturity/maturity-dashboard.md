This dashboard is an illustrative worked example showing how maturity-assessment results may be visualised, interpreted and communicated.

The authoritative maturity standard is:

`operating-model/maturity/maturity-model.md`

The authoritative assessment instrument is:

`operating-model/maturity/maturity-assessment-template.md`

This dashboard applies the maturity dimensions and levels defined by the authoritative maturity standard. It must not redefine maturity philosophy, dimensions, levels or criteria.

Where this dashboard conflicts with the authoritative maturity standard, the maturity standard takes precedence.

---

## 1. Worked Example Notice

The maturity levels, assessment results, ownership assignments, actions, priorities and review dates shown in this document are examples only.

They are not authoritative assessment results and do not represent a specific organisation, team or SC4LE implementation.

The example assumes an organisation that has established core SC4LE capabilities but has not yet reached self-sustaining maturity across all dimensions.

---

## 2. Purpose

The dashboard demonstrates how completed maturity-assessment results may be presented to:

- Central Design Authorities
- Local Design Authorities

- operating-model owners
- enablement teams
- improvement groups

It illustrates how assessment outcomes may support:

- governance decisions
- capability development
- improvement prioritisation
- investment decisions
- enablement planning
- withdrawal-readiness reviews

## 8. Visualisation Principles

Assessment results may be presented using:

- maturity summary tables
- radar charts
- heatmaps
- trend views
- governance action trackers
- capability-development roadmaps
- dimension-level evidence summaries

Visualisations should:

- use the five authoritative maturity dimensions
- use the five authoritative maturity levels
- distinguish assessment evidence from interpretation
- make example or illustrative data explicit
- avoid implying false precision
- remain accessible without relying on colour alone
- remain consistent with the authoritative maturity standard

The choice of visualisation should reflect the audience and decision being supported.

---

## 9. Example Radar Chart

A radar chart shows the maturity profile across all five dimensions in a single view.

It is particularly useful for:

- showing the balance between maturity dimensions
- comparing current and target maturity
- identifying dimensions that constrain the wider system
- supporting executive and governance discussions
- communicating the overall shape of organisational capability

### Example Radar Visualisation

![SC4LE Maturity Radar](assets/SC4LE%20Maturity%20Radar.png)

The radar chart displays the following worked-example results:

- Principles at Level 3
- Federated Governance at Level 2
- Flow and Delivery at Level 3
- AI-Enabled Sensing at Level 2
- Continuous Improvement at Level 3
- target maturity at Level 5 across all five dimensions

### Example Radar Chart Interpretation

The example radar profile shows that:

- Principles, Flow and Delivery, and Continuous Improvement are currently assessed at Level 3, Established
- Federated Governance and AI-Enabled Sensing are currently assessed at Level 2, Emerging
- Federated Governance and AI-Enabled Sensing represent the largest gaps between current and target maturity
- maturity is uneven across the five dimensions
- improvement in the two lowest dimensions may unlock wider capability development across the system

Radar charts are communication aids. The underlying evidence and maturity rationale remain within the completed maturity assessment.

A heatmap shows the maturity position of each dimension against the five authoritative maturity levels.

It is particularly useful for:

- comparing maturity dimensions

- identifying capability constraints
- highlighting improvement priorities

---

## 11. Choosing Between Radar Charts and Heatmaps

### Use a Radar Chart When

- presenting one maturity assessment
- comparing current and target maturity
- showing capability balance across all dimensions
- supporting executive discussion
- communicating the overall maturity profile

### Use a Heatmap When

- comparing multiple domains or teams
- tracking maturity over time
- identifying patterns across a portfolio
- highlighting capability concentrations and constraints
- supporting detailed governance or improvement reviews

Both visualisations may be used where they support different audience needs.

Neither visualisation changes or replaces the authoritative maturity assessment.

## 13. Usage Guidance

When creating a dashboard from a completed maturity assessment:

1. Use the five authoritative maturity dimensions.
2. Use the maturity levels recorded in the assessment.
3. Identify the evidence supporting each maturity judgement.
4. Distinguish observed findings from interpretation.
5. Highlight material constraints and dependencies.
6. Connect improvement actions to the relevant maturity dimension.
7. Identify ownership and review points.
8. State the maturity-model version used.
9. Make clear whether any information is illustrative, provisional or incomplete.
10. Choose visualisations appropriate to the audience and decision.

Dashboard reporting should support decision-making and improvement rather than become a performance-ranking or compliance exercise.
