## 1. Purpose

The SC4LE Maturity Model provides a structured, evidence-based approach for assessing how effectively an organisation has adopted and embedded the SC4LE Operating Model.

The model measures organisational capability rather than compliance.

It assesses how effectively an organisation has developed the capabilities required to apply:

- the SC4LE Principles
- Federated Governance
- Flow as a First-Class Outcome
- AI-Enabled Sensing
- Continuous Improvement as Culture

The model helps organisations:

- understand current capability
- identify systemic constraints
- prioritise capability development
- guide enablement activity
- support governance assurance
- inform leadership decision-making
- monitor organisational evolution

The model supports learning and improvement rather than judgement.

---

## 2. Relationship to the Foundations Layer

The maturity model derives its intent and design constraints from the approved Foundations artefacts:

- `foundations/sc4le-principles.md`
- `foundations/principle-interpretation-guide.md`
- `foundations/README.md`

The maturity model does not redefine the SC4LE Principles.

Instead, it assesses the extent to which the capabilities required to apply those Principles have been established, embedded and sustained within an organisation.

Where this model conflicts with an approved Foundations artefact, the Foundation artefact takes precedence.

---

## 3. What the Maturity Model Measures

The SC4LE Maturity Model measures organisational capability.

It does not measure:

- organisational size
- amount of documentation
- quantity of process
- tool complexity
- reporting volume
- governance bureaucracy

Higher maturity is demonstrated through:

- better decision quality
- stronger principle adoption
- healthier flow
- more effective governance
- improved sensing capability
- sustainable organisational ownership
- continuous adaptation and learning

---

## 4. Maturity Dimensions

The maturity model assesses five interconnected capability dimensions.

### 4.1 Principles

Assesses whether the SC4LE Principles are:

- understood
- shared across the federated system
- consistently applied
- embedded within governance and delivery
- used to resolve ambiguity and guide judgement

### 4.2 Federated Governance

Assesses:

- governance effectiveness
- clarity of decision rights
- CDA stewardship
- LDA capability
- escalation effectiveness
- governance coherence

### 4.3 Flow and Delivery

Assesses:

- flow health
- dependency management
- decision latency
- bottlenecks
- ageing work
- delivery effectiveness

### 4.4 AI-Enabled Sensing

Assesses:

- sensing capability
- quality of leading indicators
- visibility of risk and systemic conditions
- integration into decision-making
- responsible use of AI-enabled insight

### 4.5 Continuous Improvement

Assesses:

- learning behaviours
- experimentation
- improvement capability
- adaptation
- organisational evolution
- capability sustainability

The dimensions should be assessed together because weaknesses in one dimension will often constrain wider organisational capability.

---

## 5. Maturity Levels

Each dimension is assessed using five maturity levels.

### Level 1 — Foundational

Capability exists only in a limited, inconsistent or emerging form.

Characteristics typically include:

- reliance on individuals rather than shared capability
- inconsistent application
- limited governance clarity
- reactive decision-making
- low organisational confidence

### Level 2 — Emerging

Capability is developing but remains inconsistent across teams or domains.

Characteristics typically include:

- partial adoption
- improving clarity
- pockets of good practice
- growing local ownership
- inconsistent outcomes

### Level 3 — Established

Capability operates consistently and is supported by clear governance, decision-making and ways of working.

Characteristics typically include:

- reliable application
- understood decision rights
- visible outcomes
- effective governance
- repeatable practices

### Level 4 — Embedded

Capability is integrated into normal organisational behaviour and no longer relies on significant intervention or reinforcement.

Characteristics typically include:

- consistent application across domains
- reduced decision friction
- strong local ownership
- effective sensing and feedback
- behaviour aligned with the SC4LE Principles

### Level 5 — Self-Sustaining

Capability is maintained, evolved and improved through the organisation’s own governance, learning and sensing mechanisms.

Characteristics typically include:

- autonomous operation
- adaptive behaviour
- sustainable ownership
- continuous learning
- ongoing improvement without significant external enablement

## 6. Dimension Assessment Criteria

### 6.1 Principles

#### Foundational

- Principles are unclear or inconsistently understood.
- Decisions rely primarily on individuals.
- Principle-led judgement is uncommon.

#### Emerging

- Principles exist and are recognised.
- Local adoption is inconsistent.
- Escalation is frequently required.

#### Established

- Principles guide most decisions.
- Leaders reinforce them consistently.
- Teams use them to reduce ambiguity.

#### Embedded

- Principles are widely understood and applied autonomously.
- Decision quality improves through principle-led judgement.
- Principles reduce cognitive load.

#### Self-Sustaining

- Principles are naturally embedded within organisational behaviour.
- New teams adopt them rapidly.
- Principle application is reinforced through governance, sensing and continuous improvement.

---

### 6.2 Federated Governance

#### Foundational

- Governance is centralised and reactive.
- Decision rights are unclear.

#### Emerging

- Governance structures exist but remain inconsistent.
- Local authority is developing.

#### Established

- CDA and LDAs operate effectively.
- Decision pathways are understood.
- Escalation is the exception.

#### Embedded

- Federated governance is the default mode of operation.
- Governance is coherent, lightweight and effective.

#### Self-Sustaining

- Governance evolves through evidence and learning.
- Local autonomy and organisational coherence remain balanced over time.

---

### 6.3 Flow and Delivery

#### Foundational

- Bottlenecks, delays and rework are common.
- Flow is not actively managed.

#### Emerging

- Flow problems are visible but inconsistently addressed.
- Delivery relies on effort rather than system health.

#### Established

- Flow data informs improvement activity.
- Bottlenecks are regularly addressed.

#### Embedded

- Flow is treated as a first-class outcome.
- Governance actively supports delivery effectiveness.

#### Self-Sustaining

- Flow is continuously improved through learning and sensing.
- Delivery capability remains stable and predictable.

---

### 6.4 AI-Enabled Sensing

#### Foundational

- Decisions rely primarily on lagging indicators.
- Visibility is limited.

#### Emerging

- Basic sensing mechanisms exist.
- Information remains fragmented.

#### Established

- Leading indicators inform decisions.
- Governance uses sensing outputs.

#### Embedded

- Sensing is integrated into governance and delivery.
- AI-assisted insight highlights systemic patterns.

#### Self-Sustaining

- Sensing continuously improves organisational awareness.
- Insights proactively influence governance, flow and improvement.

---

### 6.5 Continuous Improvement

#### Foundational

- Improvement is reactive and inconsistent.
- Similar issues repeatedly recur.

#### Emerging

- Improvement activities exist but remain localised.
- Focus often remains on symptoms rather than causes.

#### Established

- Improvement becomes structured and evidence-based.
- Learning informs prioritisation.

#### Embedded

- Improvement is integrated into normal work.
- Learning is actively shared.

#### Self-Sustaining

- Improvement operates as a cultural norm.
- Organisational learning drives continuous adaptation and evolution.

---

## 7. Assessment Principles

Assessments should be:

- evidence-based
- proportionate
- principle-led
- transparent
- collaborative
- improvement-focused

Maturity assessment should support better outcomes rather than become a compliance exercise.

---

## 8. Use of the Maturity Model

The model may be used to support:

- organisational diagnostics
- enablement planning
- LDA establishment
- governance reviews
- operating-model assessments
- capability-development planning
- improvement prioritisation
- withdrawal readiness assessments

Assessment outcomes should inform decisions and capability development rather than simply generate scores.

## 10. Ownership and Governance

The Central Design Authority owns and stewards this model.

The CDA is responsible for:

- maintaining maturity definitions
- ensuring alignment with the Foundations layer
- maintaining alignment with the Operating Model
- reviewing assessment criteria
- approving material changes

Changes must follow applicable discovery, impact-assessment and governance requirements.
